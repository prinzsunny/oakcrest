Reading first

The year's top priority is fluent, joyful reading. The read-aloud happens every day and is never skipped.

Depth before speed

Hard problems are worth sitting with. Fast, easy work means the level is too low; tears after five minutes mean too high.

Mastery before moving on

Advance a book when practice is 85% or better without help. 'Not yet' means slow down.

Math first, screens later

The first block of the day is math on paper. Website practice is short (about 10 minutes) and comes later.

Praise effort, never 'smart'

Name the effort and the strategy. Mistakes are information, not failure.

Character before intelligence

Five virtues this year, a weekly big question, and one act of service a week.

Mathematics

Course area: Mathematics
Resources
  • Beast Academy 2A → 2B, then 2C (Guide + Practice)
  • Primary Grade Challenge Math (Zaccaro): challenge problems
  • Daily oral mental math (no calculator)
  • Soroban class practice
  • Life of Fred: Apples (a funny math story, read together twice a week)
  • Math games: Tangrams, Set, KenKen, Pentominoes, Blokus
How often
45 minutes every school day, math first; 10 minutes of mental math; 2–3 challenge problems a week; a Solution Notebook for hard problems.
How we teach it
Read the Beast Academy guide together, then work the practice book. Try for 10 minutes before asking for help. Write the week's hardest problem in the Solution Notebook: what you tried, what worked and what it taught you.
How we check
Weekly check, a monthly timed challenge set (practice, not a test), and the 85% rule before moving on. No competitions are required this year; Math Kangaroo Level 1 (March) is optional, for the experience.
Year goals
  • Beast Academy 2B complete and 2C under way
  • Addition and subtraction facts fluent to 20
  • Adds and subtracts within 100 mentally
  • Explains place value to 1,000 with real understanding
  • Solves 2-step word problems by drawing them
Sequence
Term 1 (Oct–Dec)Place value: ones, tens, hundreds; comparing and ordering; addition with regrouping and strategies.
Term 2 (Jan–Mar)Subtraction with regrouping; expressions and parentheses; balance equations; problem-solving strategies (draw it, guess and check, work backward, tables). Finish 2B.
Term 3 (Apr–Jun)Measurement and units; time and money; flexible strategies and estimation; odds and evens.
SummerMath games and puzzles a few times a week.

Reading: phonics and literature

Course area: Language Arts
Resources
  • Explode the Code Books 1–4
  • Read-alouds: The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe; Charlotte's Web; Stuart Little; Little House in the Big Woods; The Secret Garden; Mary Poppins; The Number Devil
  • Independent reading: Magic Tree House and books she chooses
  • A reading log of every book
How often
Phonics 20 minutes a day (1–2 pages); read-aloud 30–45 minutes every day, followed by narration; independent reading every day.
How we teach it
Systematic phonics builds decoding and later spelling, even for a strong reader. The parent reads aloud books 2–3 levels above her own reading; afterwards she narrates (tells back) the chapter.
How we check
Daily narration; she reads aloud to you weekly; reading log; monthly portfolio.
Year goals
  • Reads at a 2nd–3rd grade level or above, with joy
  • Decodes blends, digraphs, silent e and vowel teams
  • Reads simple chapter books (Magic Tree House level) alone
  • Reads aloud with fluency and expression
  • Explode the Code through Book 4
Sequence
Term 1Short vowels (Book 1), blends (Book 2). Read-alouds: Narnia, Charlotte's Web.
Term 2Silent e, digraphs, vowel teams (Book 3). Read-alouds: Stuart Little, Little House, The Secret Garden.
Term 3Compound words, endings, syllables (Book 4). Read-alouds: Mary Poppins, The Number Devil.
Summer15–20 books for pleasure.

Writing and grammar

Course area: Language Arts
Resources
  • Sentence Composing for Elementary School (sentence imitation)
  • First Language Lessons Level 1 (oral grammar, from month 2)
  • Daily copywork from good books
  • Daily narration; a dictated one-sentence journal
  • A weekly 10-minute freewrite (from January)
  • Monthly poetry teatime: reading poems aloud and reciting
How often
Copywork 10 minutes daily; Sentence Composing 2 × 15 minutes a week; First Language Lessons 3 × 15 minutes a week.
How we teach it
Copywork trains handwriting, spelling and the rhythm of good sentences. Narration is oral composition. Sentence imitation teaches structure by example. Grammar stays short and oral this year.
How we check
Weekly: one copywork page and one narration go in the portfolio. Monthly: compare early and recent work.
Year goals
  • Writes 2–3 sentences with correct capitals and end punctuation
  • Narrates in at least 5 complete sentences
  • Copies a sentence without letter reversals
  • Knows nouns and verbs orally
Sequence
Term 1Copywork, narration, first sentence imitations; First Language Lessons begins in month 2.
Term 2Longer copywork; narration written down by the parent and read back; nouns, verbs and describing words.
Term 3Her own 3-sentence writing about her day; capitals and punctuation on her own.

Vocabulary, word roots and Latin

Course area: Language Arts / Foreign Language
Resources
  • Word Roots Beginning
  • First Latin (in the style of Prima Latina): numbers, nature, family, animal and action words, greetings, everyday Latin
How often
Word Roots 1–2 pages a week; Latin 2 × 15 minutes a week from week 6.
How we teach it
Learn that words have roots: Latin and Greek pieces carry meaning, so unfamiliar words can be decoded. Latin words are learned with pictures and connected to the English words that come from them. This builds a strong vocabulary for later reading and tests.
How we check
Unit reviews at 90% before moving on; derivative detective games.
Year goals
  • Explains a Latin root with 5 English examples
  • Latin numbers 1–10 and about 40 everyday words
  • Knows what a.m., p.m., etc. and e.g. stand for
Sequence
Term 1What roots are; Latin numbers and greetings.
Term 2Family, nature, animal and action words; derivative detective.
Term 3Body and place words; mottos and everyday Latin; review.

Logic and chess

Course area: Mathematics / Critical Thinking
Resources
  • Building Thinking Skills Level 1
  • Mind Benders Level 1
  • Chess on a real board, plus puzzles; Chess Tactics for Students (3–5 puzzles a day from term 3)
How often
Building Thinking Skills 2–3 pages, 3 times a week; Mind Benders 3 puzzles a week; chess 15 minutes a day.
How we teach it
Figural and verbal reasoning: similarities, differences, sequences, classifications, analogies. Deduction grids. Chess for pattern recognition, patience and being a good sport.
How we check
Weekly puzzles; she explains her reasoning out loud.
Year goals
  • Solves 3×3 deduction grids alone
  • Explains an analogy
  • Knows all chess rules, including castling, and finds mate-in-one
Sequence
Term 1Similarities, differences, sequences, classifications; chess piece moves, check.
Term 2Analogies; Mind Benders; forks and back-rank mates.
Term 3Verbal reasoning; rotate and fold; full games.

Science: zoology, physics and chemistry

Course area: Science
Resources
  • Exploring Creation with Zoology 1: Flying Creatures (birds, bats, flying reptiles, insects)
  • The Story of Science: Aristotle Leads the Way (history of science)
  • Weekly hands-on physics and chemistry labs with safe home experiments
  • A nature journal on a weekly walk
  • One hands-on kit this year (a crystal-growing kit or Snap Circuits)
  • Reference shelf: DK Eyewitness Birds, Chemistry and Physics
How often
Science twice a week (30 minutes each): one zoology lesson and one lab; Story of Science read-aloud.
How we teach it
Predict, test, observe and explain why. Each lab has a short virtual experiment and a real one at home with a grown-up. Nature walks and drawing in the nature journal.
How we check
Notebooking pages; she teaches an experiment back to the family; nature journal.
Year goals
  • Names and describes 5 birds and 5 insects
  • Explains a life cycle (egg, larva, pupa, adult)
  • Knows solids, liquids and gases; float and sink; magnets; light and sound basics
  • Uses predict, test, explain
Sequence
Term 1What is zoology; birds; feathers and flight; nests. Labs: states of matter, float or sink, magnets, ramps, dissolving.
Term 2Bats; flying reptiles; insects and life cycles; social insects. Labs: sound, levers, shadows, acids and bases, fizz, density.
Term 3Butterflies; interesting insects. Labs: circuits, crystals, changes, Archimedes and Eratosthenes.

History and geography

Course area: Social Studies
Resources
  • The Story of the World Vol. 1: Ancient Times (read aloud) + Activity Guide map work
  • A children's atlas (e.g. Rand McNally), a wall world map and a timeline strip
How often
Twice a week, 30 minutes: read a chapter, narrate, map or activity.
How we teach it
History told as a story, from the earliest people to the end of Rome. She narrates each chapter and finds every place on the map.
How we check
Narration; a timeline she can explain; the map.
Year goals
  • Identifies the 7 continents and 5 oceans
  • Tells about 10 people from ancient history
  • Understands a timeline, BC and AD
Sequence
Term 1Earliest people; Egypt and the Nile; first writing; Sumer; Abraham; Hammurabi.
Term 2Assyria; India; China; Africa; Egypt's kingdoms; Moses; Phoenicians; Babylon; Crete; early Greece.
Term 3Athens and Sparta; Alexander; the Americas; Persia; Rome from republic to empire; the end of Western Rome.

Fine arts: drawing and music

Course area: Fine Arts
Resources
  • Drawing with Children (Mona Brookes)
  • Artist study: Mondrian, Kandinsky, Seurat, Matisse, Van Gogh, Kusama
  • Piano with a teacher (Faber Piano Adventures Primer); Hoffman Academy videos on practice days
  • Music theory, rhythm and ear training
How often
Drawing 2 × 20 minutes a week; piano 15 minutes daily plus the weekly lesson.
How we teach it
Observation drawing using the five elements of shape (dot, circle, straight line, curved line, angle line). Making art in the style of great artists. Daily piano practice; reading notes and rhythm.
How we check
Drawing portfolio; a home recital at the end of the year.
Year goals
  • Draws what she observes using simple shapes
  • Reads notes around middle C and simple rhythms
  • Plays a beginner recital piece
Sequence
Term 1Five elements of shape; still life. Treble clef, middle C, steady beat.
Term 2Animals and plants from life; Mondrian, Kandinsky, Seurat. Note names, rhythm, ear training.
Term 3Outdoor drawing; Matisse, Van Gogh, Kusama. Recital piece.

Character, ethics and philosophy

Course area: Health / Life Skills
Resources
  • The year's five virtues: honesty, kindness, courage, self-control, gratitude
  • The Book of Virtues (Bennett); Aesop's fables; a biography each month
  • A weekly big question (philosophy for children)
  • Feelings words
How often
A virtue moment daily; a big question once a week (15 minutes); one act of service a week.
How we teach it
Name virtues when they happen. Mistakes follow name, understand, repair, forgive. Big questions are answered with reasons ('I think… because…'), by listening to another view, and by changing your mind for a good reason.
How we check
She names the 5 virtues with examples; gives reasons in discussion; uses feelings words.
Year goals
  • Names 5 virtues and gives an example of each
  • Tells the truth when it is uncomfortable
  • Gives a reason for her view and listens to another
  • Names 15–20 feelings
Sequence
Term 1Honesty, kindness, self-control; gratitude at Thanksgiving.
Term 2Courage; owning mistakes; fairness.
Term 3All five virtues together; service; a reflection on the year.

Life skills: habits and money

Course area: Health / Life Skills
Resources
  • A morning checklist she ticks herself (the plan's 'My checklist' view)
  • An evening day review: what I did, what I learned, what I will do tomorrow
  • Give, save and spend jars with a small weekly allowance (from April)
  • The Berenstain Bears' Trouble with Money
How often
A few minutes each day; money jars once a week.
How we teach it
Executive function is built by small routines: plan, do, look back. Money is learned by handling it and making choices.
How we check
She uses her checklist with little help; she explains a give, save or spend choice.
Year goals
  • Follows a morning checklist
  • Says what she did and learned each day
  • Sorts money into give, save and spend
Sequence
Term 1–2Morning checklist and day review become habits.
Term 3Money jars and a first savings goal.
