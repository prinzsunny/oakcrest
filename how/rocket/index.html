🚀 How a rocket reaches orbit

  1. A rocket throws gas backward, and by Newton's third law the gas pushes the rocket forward with an equal force.
  2. The engine burns liquid methane with liquid oxygen (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O), and its narrow throat and widening bell speed the hot gas to over 3 km/s.
  3. The rocket leaves the ground only when thrust is greater than weight: 22 engines × 230 t just beat a 5,000 t rocket.
  4. The rocket equation, Δv = vₑ × ln(m₀ ÷ m_f), shows why a rocket is mostly propellant and why dropping an empty stage adds speed.
  5. To stay in orbit a spacecraft must fly sideways at about 7.8 km/s, so it keeps falling around the Earth instead of into it.
  6. A reusable booster flips, burns back toward home, steers with grid fins and slows with a landing burn before the tower catches it.

New words

thrust
the forward push a rocket gets from throwing its exhaust backward
specific impulse
a measure of engine efficiency: exhaust speed divided by 9.81 m/s², given in seconds
chamber pressure
how hard the burning gas presses inside the engine, about 300 times air pressure in this engine
mass ratio
the rocket's full mass divided by its empty mass, m₀ ÷ m_f
delta-v (Δv)
the total change in speed a rocket can make with the propellant it carries
gravity turn
tipping the rocket over gently after liftoff so gravity bends its path toward flying level

🏠 Try it for real

With a grown-up, thread a 3 m string through a drinking straw, tie it tight between two chairs, tape a blown-up balloon under the straw and let go. Measure how far it travels with one, two and three breaths of air.
