✏️ How a pencil sharpener works

  1. Inside the sharpener is a hole shaped like a cone, wide at the front and pointy at the back.
  2. A sharp steel blade is screwed on top, and its edge lies right along the side of the cone.
  3. When you twist the pencil, the blade shaves off a thin curl of wood, and the pencil slides a little deeper.
  4. The corners go first, then a wooden cone grows, and at the end the graphite gets a point too.
  5. The point always has the same angle as the hole, 22 degrees, so more turns only make the pencil shorter.

New words

cone
a shape that is round at one end and comes to a point at the other, like an ice cream cone
blade
a thin, very sharp piece of metal that cuts
angle
how wide two lines open apart where they meet
graphite
the dark middle of a pencil that makes the marks
shaving
a thin curl that gets cut off

🏠 Try it for real

With a grown-up, look at a hand sharpener and find the little screw that holds the blade, then sharpen a pencil and count your turns until it is pointy.

💭 Talk about it

Why does a pencil get shorter every time you sharpen it, and where does the missing part go?
